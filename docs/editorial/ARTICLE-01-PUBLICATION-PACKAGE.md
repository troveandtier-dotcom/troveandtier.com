## 1. Editorial promise

Help readers understand their kitchen before buying another organizer. The article's core sequence is audit → measure → zone → adapt → buy only if a real gap remains.

**Search intent:** a reader with a cramped kitchen wants a method for organizing it before spending money.

## 5. Primary-source and editorial verification notes

- [National Fire Protection Association — Cooking safety](https://www.nfpa.org/education-and-research/home-fire-safety/cooking): advises keeping anything that can catch fire, including oven mitts, wooden utensils, food packaging, towels and curtains, away from the stovetop. The article links to this official page.
- [Indiana Department of Environmental Management — Safe storage and use of hazardous household products](https://www.in.gov/idem/health/household-hazardous-wastes/safe-storage-and-use-of-hazardous-household-products/): supports the statements about not storing hazardous products in food or beverage containers or in the same area as food, keeping them out of reach of children and pets, and not mixing household chemicals. The article links to this official state-agency page.
- NKBA aisle-width figures were removed. The article now gives a qualitative clearance test and says building codes and planning guidance vary by country and locality.
- Waist-to-shoulder reach, heavy items low and leaving spare capacity are presented as practical editorial advice, not technical standards.
- Ready.gov attribution and unsupported claims were removed.
- No product testing, product endorsements, prices, availability or affiliate links are claimed.
