# Trove & Tier — Story-first editorial and marketing standard

## Strategic principle
Lead with the lived moment the reader wants, not a list of product features. Make the reader recognize a real situation, picture a better version of it, and understand why a particular idea or product may help.

## The four-part message
1. **Recognizable moment:** a specific routine, friction point, decision or small pleasure. Use concrete details, not generic emotional claims.
2. **Desired experience:** describe what becomes easier, calmer, clearer, more comfortable or more enjoyable.
3. **Practical mechanism:** explain the actual feature, material, dimensions, design or method that makes the improvement plausible.
4. **Fit and trade-offs:** state who it suits, who it does not, constraints, maintenance, alternatives and what the reader should verify.

## Where to apply it
- Homepage: communicate the kind of home moments Trove & Tier helps readers improve.
- Category pages: open with the room's recognizable routine and the decisions that make it work better.
- Journal: use a concrete problem or scene in the opening, then deliver practical, evidence-aware guidance. Do not force a narrative into every section.
- Product cards: lead with the use case and fit; retain specifications and limitations nearby.
- Product pages: show the everyday context, then substantiate with verified product facts, dimensions, materials, compatibility and drawbacks.
- Pinterest/social: use a vivid but truthful moment as the hook; give a useful takeaway and accurate destination.
- Partnerships: sell editorial understanding and audience usefulness, never invented reach, sales or customer testimonials.

## House rules
- Story is an entry point, not a substitute for information. Readers still need specs, price freshness, fit, alternatives, safety and clear disclosures.
- Never invent a customer, quote, review, test, personal experience, result, demographic, partnership or product capability.
- Do not imply Trove & Tier physically tested a product unless documented testing actually occurred.
- Do not use fear, shame, false scarcity, exaggerated transformation or manipulative urgency.
- Avoid empty lifestyle clichés such as "elevate your space" unless followed by a concrete explanation.
- Use sensory and situational detail only when plausible and relevant; do not fabricate a reader's exact home or circumstances.
- Preserve accessibility: do not imply that a better home requires buying more or spending more.
- Every commercial recommendation must include fit, limitations and a no-purchase or lower-cost alternative where useful.
- Keep market-specific claims, retailer details and affiliate disclosures accurate.

## Editorial test
Before approval, ask:
- Can a reader recognize the moment in the first few lines?
- Is the desired improvement concrete rather than vague?
- Have we explained the mechanism, not just promised a feeling?
- Are facts, specifications and claims supported?
- Are limitations and alternatives visible?
- Does the story still help someone who buys nothing?

## Brand expression
Trove & Tier helps people make more of the home they already have, then choose additions thoughtfully. The feeling is considered, useful and quietly optimistic—not perfection, pressure or shopping for its own sake.
